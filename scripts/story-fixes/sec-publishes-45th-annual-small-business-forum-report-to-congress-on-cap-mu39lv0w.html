WASHINGTON — The Securities and Exchange Commission on July 27, 2026, released its report to Congress on the 45th Annual Government-Business Forum on Small Business Capital Formation, designated release No. 2026-70. The report summarizes the March 9, 2026, forum, the policy recommendations participants developed, and the Commission's responses to those recommendations.

It is a report to Congress, not a mid-September rulemaking and not a "Monday" release. The SEC's July 27 press statement is the originating notice; the underlying document is the 2026 forum report the Commission delivered that day.

Strategic Context

Congress charged the SEC's Office of the Advocate for Small Business Capital Formation with hosting the annual forum, where officials and private-sector participants discuss how entrepreneurs, small businesses, and smaller public companies raise capital. The March 9 session, at SEC headquarters in Washington and by webcast, included remarks from each of the commissioners.

The Commission's July 27 notice listed three session topics: Early-Stage Capital Raising; Growth-Stage Companies and Smaller Funds; and Small Cap Companies and the Public Markets. The forum report's proceedings use matching panels — "Funding for Founders: Empowering Early-Stage Entrepreneurs," "Investing in Innovation: Supporting Growth-Stage Companies," and "Public Company Perspectives: Considerations for IPOs and Small Caps" — with 12 outside speakers alongside the commissioners.

Industry & Analyst Perspectives

The recommendations in the report were developed by 2026 forum participants. The report states that those recommendations are not endorsed by the SEC and do not necessarily reflect the views of the Commission or its staff. That is the opposite of an analyst roundup: it is a participant list, ranked by attendees after each panel, then answered by the agency.

The report prints the top five recommendations from each of the three lifecycle segments and, beside each item, a Commission response that notes any corresponding policy initiative already made public. Sixteen recommendations were prioritized for the Commission and Congress; one growth-stage slot is a tie, so the printed list is slightly longer than 15. Early-stage items include expanding the accredited-investor definition to additional sophistication tests and raising the Regulation Crowdfunding annual cap from $5 million to $20 million. Small-cap items include expanding Form S-3 eligibility regardless of public float. In each case the Commission said it will consider the recommendation in connection with exempt-offering updates, the 2026 Regulatory Agenda, or other open initiatives — not that this report itself changes a rule.

Financial & Macro Implications

Release 2026-70 does not amend Regulation D, Regulation Crowdfunding, Form S-3, or the cost of being a smaller reporting company. What it does is put participant asks and Commission replies in one congressional filing. Where the Commission already has a public initiative — for example exempt-offering work on the 2026 Regulatory Agenda, or the May 19, 2026, registered-offering and filer-status proposals cited in the report's small-cap responses — the reply points there. Where it does not, the reply is that the Commission will consider the item. That is the capital-formation content of the document. It is not a forecast of private-market volumes or the cost of capital.

Forward Outlook

The Commission said it will consider forum recommendations alongside other public comments on relevant policy initiatives. Video archives and a transcript of the March 9 discussions are posted with the forum materials. The next facts to watch are those agenda items and proposals the Commission already cited in its responses — not a new statutory clock started by the July 27 mailing.
