SAN FRANCISCO — Salesforce Inc. chief executive Marc Benioff warned companies building artificial intelligence not to repeat the social-media era's harms, telling CNBC's Jim Cramer in a "Mad Money" interview from the sidelines of Dreamforce that "a lot of companies got hurt, a lot of individuals got hurt through social media" and "we don't want that to happen in AI."

The remarks, reported by CNBC on Sept. 15, 2026, land in a sharper safety debate after Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei published a weekend essay urging frontier labs to slow the pace of model development so safety work can catch up. Benioff joined that debate. He stopped short of calling for a slowdown.

Strategic Context

Benioff has spent years arguing that social platforms inflicted costs the industry was slow to own. In 2018 he called Facebook "the new cigarettes" and pressed for heavier regulation. CNBC framed the Dreamforce interview as the same instinct applied to AI: treat social-media harms as a cautionary tale, and do not wait until the damage is obvious.

The setting matters. Dreamforce is Salesforce's annual customer event in San Francisco. Last month the company unveiled Claudeforce, including a plugin that lets clients use Anthropic's Claude on customer data stored in Salesforce — composing email, updating records, and similar tasks. On Tuesday, Amodei appeared on stage during Benioff's keynote and said, "I think that's the way to lead the industry forward, to set an example, to say that everyone can always be better."

Industry & Analyst Perspectives

CNBC did not quote sell-side analysts on the interview. The named voices in its report are Benioff, Cramer, and Amodei. That is the useful split. Amodei's essay asked frontier labs to ease off the accelerator. Benioff, sitting with a close Anthropic partner, argued instead that the companies shipping the technology have to "be held responsible" while they keep building. "The heart of ethics is responsibility," he told Cramer.

He added, "We really look at a light on our brethren here in San Francisco and all over the world and say, 'We want you to be ethical in your actions and how you take care of your communities and the world,' because this technology is very powerful." CNBC reported that he stopped short of criticizing AI companies or endorsing calls to slow development.

Financial & Macro Implications

CNBC reported that Salesforce shares on Tuesday gave back some of their more than 4.5% advance from the prior session. Enterprise software stocks, the same report said, had soared Monday after Amodei's call to slow development — a bounce in a group that was "crushed earlier this year on AI disruption fears" and has been recovering as investors warmed to the idea that AI cannot replace everything. Salesforce was up more than 50% from a 52-week low of $146 in late June, CNBC said, and remains a long-time holding in the CNBC Investing Club portfolio.

Those are tape facts from the source. They are not a forecast of Salesforce margins, sales-cycle length, or AI-feature attach rates. CNBC did not report those figures, and this desk is not inventing them.

Forward Outlook

The immediate watch is whether other enterprise-software chiefs echo Amodei's slowdown argument or Benioff's version: keep shipping, and treat ethics as an operating obligation. Salesforce's own Claudeforce launch and Amodei's keynote appearance are the concrete pairing to track — a vendor warning about AI harm while deepening a tie to a frontier lab.
